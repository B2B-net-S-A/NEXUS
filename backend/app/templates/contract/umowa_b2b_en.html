§ 11

Force Majeure

1. Force majeure shall mean an external event beyond the control of the Party, which could not have been foreseen despite the exercise of due diligence and which could not have been prevented or overcome, and which prevents, in whole or in part, the performance of obligations under the Agreement, in particular: natural disasters, acts of war, riots, acts of public authorities, power outages, failures of information and communication systems or communication networks.

2. A Party that is unable to perform or properly perform its obligations under the Agreement due to force majeure shall not be liable for non-performance or improper performance of the Agreement to the extent that such non-performance or improper performance is directly related to the occurrence of force majeure.

3. A Party invoking force majeure is obligated to immediately notify the other Party of its occurrence, but no later than 3 days after the event occurs, of the anticipated impact on the performance of the Agreement, and, to the extent possible, to provide documents or other credible evidence confirming the occurrence of such circumstances.

4. Failure to notify or delay in notifying the other Party of the occurrence of force majeure shall result in that Party being unable to effectively invoke force majeure as a ground for exemption from liability for non-performance or improper performance of the Agreement.

5. The party affected by force majeure is obligated to take measures to mitigate its effects and to resume performance of its contractual obligations without delay once the force majeure circumstances have ceased.

6. If the force majeure event lasts longer than 15 days, either Party may terminate the Agreement with immediate effect, without the right to claim damages from the other Party on this account.
